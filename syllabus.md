## Course description

This course provides an overview of the theory and practice of human-computer interaction. Students will develop an appreciation for designing usable interfaces and gain the skills to conduct heuristic evaluations and think-aloud usability tests.

### Learning outcomes

After successfully completing the course, you will be able to:

- explain core concepts and the historical foundations of HCI;
- apply user research to understand people, tasks, and contexts of use;
- design interaction flows and visual interfaces with usability in mind;
- create and iterate on prototypes using modern HTML and CSS;
- inspect interfaces with usability heuristics; and
- plan, conduct, and communicate a think-aloud usability test.

## Course content

1. Human-Computer Interaction
2. The Psychology of Usable Things
3. Usability Engineering
4. User Research
5. Usability Benchmarking
6. Interaction Design
7. Prototyping
8. Usability Inspection Methods
9. Usability Testing Methods
10. Usability in Practice
11. Visual Design and Typography
12. Icon Design
13. Early History of HCI

## Format and preparation

The course is taught as a lecture with integrated practical exercises (1 lecture / 2 exercise hours). Practical work assumes knowledge of modern HTML and CSS. Dates, rooms, exercise instructions, and submission details will be published in TeachCenter.

## Course text

The course uses [*Introduction to HCI*](https://introductiontohci.org/) as its textbook. Assigned sections and any additional readings will be announced with the relevant class sessions.

## Assessment

Assessment is continuous. Details on exercises, deliverables, deadlines, and grading will be published in TeachCenter.

## Academic integrity

All submitted work must be your own and written in your own words. Do not copy from elsewhere. The use of generative AI tools is not allowed.
